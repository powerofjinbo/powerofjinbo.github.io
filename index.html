About

I am an undergraduate physics student at the University of California, Irvine, applying to PhD programs in experimental high-energy physics for Fall 2027. My research centers on collider physics, dark-matter searches, and machine learning for particle physics — in particular, data-driven methods that extend the reach of precision measurements and searches for new physics.

In summer 2026, I was a SURF fellow at Caltech with Profs. Bertrand Echenard and David Hitlin, where I designed and simulated a muon-beam missing-momentum experiment to search for light dark matter. I currently work with Prof. Christoph Paus at MIT on the projected precision of Higgs-boson measurements at the proposed FCC-ee collider, and with Prof. Daniel Whiteson at UCI I led the development of a time-dependent resonance search for new physics at the LHC, now submitted to Physical Review D.

Earlier in my undergraduate career, I worked in observational astronomy with Prof. Asantha Cooray, analyzing Herschel-selected dusty star-forming galaxies with ALMA and Spitzer data, and in nanophotonics with Prof. Howard Lee, fabricating gate-tunable 2D ITO epsilon-near-zero materials. Outside research, I serve as a Learning Assistant for upper-division classical mechanics and electromagnetism, and as webmaster of the Society of Physics Students at UCI.

Research

  • Muon-Beam Dark Matter Search
    Caltech SURF · Profs. Bertrand Echenard & David Hitlin · June – Aug. 2026

    Designed and simulated an LDMX-style muon-beam missing-momentum experiment for light dark matter: a detailed Geant4 model of a 40-layer LYSO crystal active target with per-crystal energy-deposition readout, with the full analysis chain run at the 108-muon scale on a Slurm cluster. I characterized the rare backgrounds that mimic the signal — muon bremsstrahlung with photonuclear interactions and hard muon–nucleus scattering — and combined calorimetric vetoes with BDT, graph-neural-network, and transformer classifiers, reaching ∼90% signal efficiency with zero surviving background events under a preregistered statistical-testing protocol. A sole-authored manuscript is in final preparation for arXiv.

  • Higgs Precision Measurements at FCC-ee
    MIT · Prof. Christoph Paus · Jan. 2026 – Present

    An end-to-end sensitivity study of σ(ZH) × B(H → WW*) in the semileptonic qq̄ ℓν qq̄′ final state at √s = 240 GeV, processing over 109 fully simulated events across 32 signal and background processes with the FCCAnalyses C++/RDataFrame framework. I designed a two-category measurement separating on-shell and off-shell leptonic-W decay topologies (95–98% category purity), trained an independent XGBoost classifier per category (test AUC 0.99), and implemented profile-likelihood inference in pyhf, projecting a 0.55% precision on the inclusive signal strength.

  • Time-Dependent New Physics at the LHC
    UC Irvine · Prof. Daniel Whiteson · May 2025 – May 2026

    Led the development of a time-dependent resonance search strategy that introduces event time as an auxiliary feature, improving sensitivity beyond mass-only searches. We extended CATHODE-based anomaly detection to model backgrounds in invariant-mass and time sidebands, simulated signal and background processes with MadGraph5, Pythia8, and Delphes, and quantified the sensitivity gains through classifier performance, sideband closure diagnostics, and expected upper limits on the signal strength.

    arXiv:2605.11071 · Poster

  • High-Redshift Galaxy Evolution
    UC Irvine · Prof. Asantha Cooray · Dec. 2024 – Sep. 2025

    Analyzed photometric data from the Spitzer and ALMA telescopes to constrain the redshift distribution and physical properties of Herschel-selected ultra-red dusty star-forming galaxies, and developed automated pipelines in Python and CARTA to visualize spectral energy distributions and interpret high-redshift signals.

  • Epsilon-Near-Zero Nanophotonics
    UC Irvine · Prof. Howard Lee · Feb. 2024 – Sep. 2025

    Fabricated gate-tunable two-dimensional indium tin oxide (ITO) films by liquid-metal printing for epsilon-near-zero applications, and characterized the optical nonlinearity and tunability of ENZ metasurfaces for next-generation nanophotonic devices.

    Proc. SPIE · Poster
